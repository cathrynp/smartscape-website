How to Help Birds Survive a Heat Wave (Hint: It's Not Just Water)

By Cathryn Poff

Heat waves are getting worse — and birds are taking a real hit. Ornithologist Blair Wolf, who's studied this for decades, explains it simply: birds cool down by panting, the same way a dog does. Panting moves air over the moist lining of their mouth and lungs, and as that moisture evaporates, it pulls heat out of their body. It works — but it burns through their water fast. If it's hot and there's no water to replace what they've lost, they die of dehydration.

This isn't rare anymore — it's a growing part of the climate story.

The good news: your yard can help birds through it every summer. Here's what matters most:

Water and shade, first

  • Birds also cool off simply by getting wet and letting the water evaporate from their feathers. So a bird bath — even a simple one — gives birds another way to cool off directly, and a place to drink when natural water sources dry up.
  • Native trees and shrubs give birds shady spots to wait out the hottest part of the day

Why they have to be native plants specifically

  • Native species are built for your region's climate extremes — they keep providing shade and food during a heat wave, while non-native plants are more likely to wilt or stall without supplemental watering, right when it matters
  • 96% of North American songbirds rely on caterpillars to feed their young — and those caterpillars need native plants to survive. No natives, no caterpillars, no bird food
  • Bonus: native yards need way less mowing and blowing — so one less source of the emissions driving all this in the first place

The takeaway: a bird bath plus some native trees and shrubs do more for birds in a heat wave than almost anything else you could do in your own yard.
